# PokeSquad Battler

A phone-first squad battler set in Kanto with every Pokémon from Kanto to Paldea (1,025 species, and the forms with their own stats). It is built on the same engine as Melty Blood RPG and Arknights Tactics Mobile, re-themed so that every term is Pokémon terminology. It's an unofficial, non-commercial fan game: Pokémon © Nintendo, Game Freak and Creatures Inc. The Pokémon sprites (the animated Black/White ones, front and back) and the trainer sprites come from [Pokémon Showdown](https://play.pokemonshowdown.com/sprites/), and the species, move and ability data from [PokeAPI](https://pokeapi.co/).

Open `PokeSquad Battler.html` directly, or serve the folder (`npm run serve`, then `http://localhost:8000/PokeSquad%20Battler.html`). It's a single file: the sprites are embedded.

**Online battles** happen on PokeSquad Battler's own Pokémon Showdown site, **https://mikale4.github.io/PokeSquad-Showdown-Client/**: a Showdown server running the game's own battle engine, with Showdown's teambuilder on the game's data, the turn meter, a ladder, registered names and shared replays (see *Online battles*, below). The game's own online page (`online.html`) was removed on 2026-10-20, the owner's pick.

`PokeSquad Battler Reference.html` lists every move, Ability, item and TM with what it does in this game (the game's own descriptions), who learns or has it and where to get it, with search, filters (type, category, slot, Pokémon, source...) and sorting. It's generated from the built game: after a rebuild, run `node tools/build_reference.js`.
Every row has a **Note** button: write what you'd like changed, then **Notes → Export new notes** puts them in the `Notes` folder for Claude (see `Notes/README.md`); handled notes show **✓ Done** with Claude's reply.

## Game Boy shell

The whole game runs on a Game Boy screen. Its shape is a setting (Settings → Game Boy → Shape, kept per device): **Gameboy** is the upright DMG-style body and **Switch** the wide Game Boy Advance-style one with the controls either side of the screen. Both are laid out at a phone's size (390×844 upright, 844×390 sideways) and scaled as one picture, screen contents and all, so they look the same on every screen: the Gameboy as tall as the window, the Switch filling a phone and, in a bigger window, sitting in the middle at up to 86% of it and 1180px wide. and **Hybrid** (the default) picks by the screen's shape (wider than 5:4 gets the wide body, filling the window), so a phone turns between them and a desktop window follows its size. The buttons work:

| Button | Menus | Battle |
|---|---|---|
| D-pad | left/right switches tabs, up/down scrolls | left/right picks the target, up/down highlights a move |
| A | presses the screen's main button, advances text | uses the highlighted move |
| B | back / close | shows the target's info |
| START | the START menu (Pokédex, Pokémon, Bag, Trainer Card, Mail, Save, Option) | pause |
| SELECT | the Bag | Auto on/off |

Keyboard: arrows, Z or Space (A), X or Esc (B), Enter (START), Shift (SELECT). The Trainer Card's options change the frame colour (Classic, Pikachu, Red, Blue, Atomic Purple) or turn it off. The shell lives in `a9d_gameboy.js` (logic) and at the end of `a1_head.html` (CSS); everything else is positioned inside `#screen`, a size container, so screen CSS uses `cqh`/`cqw` instead of `vh`/`vw`.

## What's in it

- **1,025 Pokémon** from Bulbasaur to Pecharunt, plus 104 forms that are Pokémon of their own (the Alolan, Galarian, Hisuian and Paldean forms, Rotom's, the Therians, Oricorio, Lycanroc, Dusk Mane and Dawn Wings Necrozma, Crowned Zacian and Zamazenta, Calyrex's riders, Ogerpon's masks...) and 13 battle-only forms (Zen Mode, School Wishiwashi, Minior's Core, Complete Zygarde, Blade Aegislash, Noice Eiscue, Hero Palafin, Terastal Terapagos, Castform's weather forms, Pirouette Meloetta). No Megas, Z-Moves, Dynamax or Tera. Current types and base stats, and **moves from every generation** with their current power, type and accuracy (all 818 moves Pokémon can learn, each with a battle rule). A Pokémon knows one move per slot:
  - **S1** has no cooldown: the light attacks (up to about 55 power: Ember, Water Gun, Quick Attack, Razor Leaf...) and every 60-power attack (Wing Attack, Aerial Ace, Water Pulse...; the owner's rule, 2026-10-16), but never one that can flinch (Astonish, Bite, Heart Stamp, Needle Arm, Rolling Kick, Snore and Twister are S2: the owner's rule, 2026-10-19).
  - **S2** has a cooldown: mid-power attacks and every status move (Slash, Body Slam, Thunder Wave, Swords Dance, Protect...).
  - **S3** has a longer cooldown: the big ones (90 power and up, Explosion, Hyper Beam, Super Fang and the moves like it).
  - Pokémon learn moves by level like in the games: an empty slot takes the move, otherwise "Charmander wants to learn Metal Claw!" asks whether to forget the move in that slot. Evolving learns what the new species learns at that level.
  - **The Move Reminder** (Moves tab) teaches any level-up move a Pokémon could know by its level, for Poké Dollars (S1 500, S2 1,000, S3 2,000).
  - **The Move Tutor** (Moves tab, from stage 4-3 of your first Journey; the owner's pick, 2026-10-20) teaches every main game's **egg moves and move-tutor moves** its species can learn, one **Heart Scale** each (the Poké Mart 5,000 Poké Dollars, the BP Exchange 40 BP, and now and then in Explore). The Demo uses it for its planned sets, and the Trainers' builds take these moves from Lv 30 (`npcAccess`).
  - Level-up learnsets combine every generation (when games disagree, the newest one's level counts). PokeAPI's data (`tools/build_data.py`) is filled in from Pokémon Showdown's learnsets (`tools/showdown_learnsets.json`, made by `tools/build_showdown_learnsets.js` from the Showdown fork): the level-up and TM moves PokeAPI missed (Ceruledge's Lv 1 Shadow Sneak...), and every game's egg and tutor moves (each species' `eg` and `tu`; a TM with no number in our lists, like an Omega Ruby/Alpha Sapphire-only one, is a tutor move). Event and transfer moves stay out.
  - **TMs:** every generation's TMs, HMs and TRs (955), named by generation where numbers repeat: TM01 (Gen 1) is Mega Punch, TM01 (Gen 2) Dynamic Punch. The Poké Mart's TM counter sells six at a time, a new six every 15 minutes (S1 1,500, S2 2,500, S3 4,000). Each teaches its move once to a Pokémon that could learn it in that generation; spares wait in the TM Case.
  - A Pokémon with no S1 move (Abra, whose only move is Teleport) uses Struggle when nothing else is ready. Every move a Pokémon can learn is in; the ones whose games' rules lean on PP, positions or a chosen order work as listed under House rules.
- **Abilities:** every Ability a species has had in any generation (Gengar: Levitate or Cursed Body), each with a working battle effect. A caught Pokémon gets one of its regular Abilities at random; the **Ability Capsule** (150 BP) switches to another regular one and the **Ability Patch** (400 BP) to its Hidden Ability, both from the BP Exchange and used on the Summary.
- **Natures:** all 25 (+10% to one stat, −10% to another; the Summary marks them ▲▼). Caught Pokémon get a random one; the Poké Mart sells **Mints** (10,000 each) that change it.
- **Held items:** 211, all working in battle (Silvally's 17 Memories, Booster Energy for the Paradox Pokémon, the Adamant, Lustrous and Griseous Orbs, the Soul Dew and Ogerpon's masks among them): type boosters, Plates, Gems and Incenses; every status, HP, pinch and type-resist Berry; Choice items, Life Orb, Focus Sash, Rocky Helmet, Weakness Policy, Air Balloon, Eject Button and Pack, Red Card, Heavy-Duty Boots, weather rocks, Light Clay, terrain seeds, Lucky Egg (+50% Exp.), Amulet Coin (double Poké Dollars) and more. Berries come from the Poké Mart, battle items from the BP Exchange, the rest from Explore. Mega Stones, Z-Crystals and Tera/Dynamax items are left out (no such mechanics here).
- **House rules** (where this game differs from the main series on purpose):
  - Choice items boost without locking a move, so the **Item Clause** (the team config's `rules.itemClause`) allows one each of Choice Band, Choice Specs, Choice Scarf, Life Orb, Muscle Band, Wise Glasses, Assault Vest, Booster Energy, Berserk Gene, Ring Target, Quick Claw, King's Rock and Razor Fang per team (King's Rock and Razor Fang since 2026-10-19: one of each). The team screen won't start a battle that breaks it; in drafts and opposing teams a second copy simply doesn't work.
  - **Light Ball** (the owner's rule): it works for the whole Pikachu line, Pichu, Pikachu, Raichu and Alolan Raichu (Attack and Sp. Atk +2), not only for Pikachu. Red's Raichu at the top of Mt. Silver holds one.
  - **Waves** (the owner's rules): every stage fields its waves as one team, three at a time, the rest coming in as they faint, wild stages and Explore too. (From 2026-10-15 a normal stage's waves came one at a time, as new battles, and a first chapter's wild stages one Pokémon a wave with softer aces; on 2026-10-16 the owner took those handicaps out of every chapter, as the Professor's gifts cover the early game. Sprites, sounds and the Pokédex data load from Showdown's own site. Its `POKESQUAD.md` explains the fork.
- **The teambuilder on PokeSquad's data** (phase 2, 2026-10-20): `build.sh` also writes the client fork's `pokesquad/data.js` (`tools/build_showdown_data.js`, from the engine bundle and the server fork's built dex, then `tools/showdown_data_loader.js`, which lays it over Showdown's data once that has loaded, as the mod `gen9pokesquad`: the format's `isPokeSquad` in the fork's `battle-dex-data.ts`). The [Gen 9] PokeSquad teambuilder lists only the game's 1,130 Pokémon (by generation, the PvP groups as their tier: Legend, Mythical, UB, Paradox), each one's moves from `learnPool` (level-up, TMs, egg and tutor moves, nothing borrowed from a pre-evolution), its Abilities (Hidden ones too) and the game's held items, with the game's numbers and texts (each move's slot in place of PP; Sketch, Copycat and Mirror Move in S1 and S3 as "Sketch (S1)"...). A set has three move boxes, S1, S2 and S3: each lists its own slot's moves, a typed move goes to its own slot, and two moves for one slot show below for the server to turn down. Stats are the game's (Lv 100, every IV 28, no EVs: only the nature to pick); no Tera. Battles show the game's numbers and texts in the tooltips too.
- **The turn meter on Showdown's battle screen** (phase 3, 2026-10-20): the engine bundle sends `|custom|-pokesquadmeter|` lines (whose turn it is, each Pokémon's meter, who acts next: `meterLine` in `tools/showdown_adapter.js`); the client fork draws a meter bar under each HP bar (gold for the Pokémon whose turn it is), the next seven to act as icons in the right sidebar (blue underline: your side, red: the other), and the meter in each Pokémon's tooltip. Its page loads its own battle scene (`js/battle-animations.js`) instead of Showdown's `data/graphics.js`.
- **What the screen shows**: the server runs the game's own engine, so every move, Ability and held item works exactly as in the game (Lv 100, top IVs, no Badges: the format's rules); the translator also draws the screens, Tailwind and the rooms on Showdown's field (`syncSides`) and shows held items used up or changed hands (`syncItems`). Showdown's English text file can load twice: the data loader lays the game's texts back over it (`applyTexts`). A lower HP bar is drawn over the one above it, so every name shows.
- **The rest of phase 3** (2026-10-20): battle tooltips go by the game's rules (a move: its power, accuracy, slot and cooldown and the game's text, none of Showdown's modifiers; an opponent's Speed by nature only, every IV 28 and no EVs; your own stats with their stat stages only, as the game turns items, Abilities and statuses into stat stages); move buttons show each move's slot, or how long until it's ready, where Showdown shows PP (the request's `slot`/`cooldown`, each move by its site id: `sdMove`); the Ladder page shows the server's own ladder (`Config.localladder`: `/cmd laddertop`); a downloaded replay plays on the site's own player (`Config.replayembed`: its `js/replay-embed.js` loads the site's battle scene, tooltips and `pokesquad/data.js`), and **Upload and share replay** saves the battle in the server's database and gives a link to the site's `replay.html#<id>`, which plays it on the same player. The ladder lives in a free Postgres database (the owner's pick, 2026-10-20: `DATABASE_URL` in Render's Environment settings, the owner's own; the fork's `server/pokesquad-db.ts`), since Render's free plan wipes the server's disk whenever it sleeps or redeploys; without it the ladder stays in a file and resets. The same database keeps registered names (there's no Showdown login server): the gear button's **Register** sets a password for the name you're using, kept only as a salted hash; choosing a registered name then asks for it, and the browser remembers the login for 30 days (the fork's `server/chat-plugins/pokesquad-names.ts`, `checkName`). The names in `POKESQUAD_ADMINS` (the fork's `render.yaml`: the owner's **admin**) are administrators (Showdown's `~` rank) once logged in with their password.
- **Players:** pick **[Gen 9] PokeSquad**, build or import a team (Showdown's teambuilder; the server checks it by PokeSquad's rules: at most one move per slot, an S1 move, the PvP limits, the Item Clause), then **Battle!** or challenge someone. Showdown's ladder never pairs two players from the same IP address, so two on the same Wi-Fi challenge each other.
